A quarter of the manufacturing workforce is over 55. In 2000 it was 14%.

That single shift explains more about the last three years of factory software buying than any AI roadmap does.

The numbers underneath it are worse than the headline. 26% of the current workforce is expected to retire by 2030, leaving around 1,5 million roles unfilled. 75% of manufacturers already report a skill deficit. 97% say they are worried about the brain drain of people walking out the door.

Frost and Sullivan names the three drivers of this whole category as labour shortages, aging workforce demographics and rising operational complexity. Notice that two of the three are demographic. None of them is "the models got better".

Here is what that means in practice.

When a 30 year veteran retires, their replacement inherits the documented part of what that person knew. The rest goes with them: the feel of a properly tensioned belt, which line runs hot in July, which supplier to call at 6am when a part is missing. None of it is in the SOP. Most of it was never written down because the person who knew it never needed to write it down.

So the question on the table is not whether AI can help. It is whether you are capturing what your best operators know while they are still on the floor to ask.

Every year you wait, the cohort gets smaller.

What is your plant doing about it?
