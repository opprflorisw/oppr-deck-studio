The best manufacturing post I read this month was a man proving he was not an AI.

A machine shop owner in Japan had been posting photos of his work. Enough people assumed the posts were generated that he filmed himself in the shop, standing at the wire EDM that cuts his lantern cages, and added subtitles because his English is not strong.

It drew 2.359 upvotes. Not for the parts. For being demonstrably a person.

Someone asked about the machine. His answer:

"Good eye. Yes, it is an older Mitsubishi. We put money into maintenance every year to keep it running, so it still holds tolerance and works every day. No reason to replace it."

Two lines, and both of them are worth reading twice if you sell anything into a factory.

The first is about trust. This audience treats polished, generated-looking content as a warning sign, not as professionalism. The credibility move is the opposite of what marketing usually optimises for: rough footage, a real machine, a name, a face.

The second is about buying. He maintains a machine that is decades old because it holds tolerance and works every day. That is not resistance to technology. That is a completely rational standard, and it is the standard anything new gets measured against. Not "is it modern". Does it hold, and does it work every day.

I spend a lot of time around software that wants to sit on a shop floor. The lesson from a thread that had nothing to do with software is the one I keep coming back to.

Earn it like the Mitsubishi did.
